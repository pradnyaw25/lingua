About Lingua

A personal workshop for reading my way into French and Spanish.

Why I built it

I've been learning Spanish and French, on and off, for more than ten years — Duolingo, in-person classes, Netflix with the subtitles on. All of it kept me going, but I could never quite make the leap past it, especially when it came to reading and picking up new vocabulary.

What finally helped was reading with the translation right beside the text. The problem was that I was doing it by hand: copying sentence after sentence into Google Translate as I went. I wanted texts that simply came with the English alongside, so I could just read. And on the rare occasions I found a parallel-text site, the content was dated, open-license material I had no real interest in reading.

So I built Lingua — a quiet place to read something worth reading with the English right beside it, tap any word I don't know, and drill the vocabulary that actually comes up. No copy-pasting into Google Translate, no accounts, no ads. It's a plain static site, so it loads instantly and I can add a new text just by dropping in a file, and I keep tinkering: original short stories, the 1,000 most common words, even a small new story generated each day.

Who it's for

It's aimed at advanced-beginner to intermediate learners — roughly A2 to B2. That's the stage I got stuck at: past the basics, ready to read real text, but still reaching for the dictionary on every other line.

What's inside

Three parts — a parallel-text reader where you tap any word for its meaning, the 1,000 most common words in each language sorted into quick categories, and flashcards over any set.
